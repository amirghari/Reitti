# Reitti — Test & Questionnaire Catalog

*Working document. The core content layer of the app. Everything clinical here is provisional until the clinician co-founder signs off. No actual test item wording is reproduced here — this is a register of instruments, what they measure, their scoring, licensing, and how they feed routing.*

---

## The taxonomy — two clinical layers, one optional engagement layer

Your two-type idea is right. Naming them clearly matters, because the *purpose* of each layer sets the rigor bar.

- **Type 1 — "Find your path" (screening / routing).** Short, validated screeners whose only job is to help place a person on the right rung of care. Output = severity band + a routing signal. Never a diagnosis, never a label.
- **Type 2 — "Track your progress" (measurement-based care).** Instruments a person takes over time, owns, and can share with a therapist to make sessions sharper and to see change. Output = score + trend.
- **Type 3 — "Understand yourself" (optional, engagement).** Popular, shareable self-understanding tests (personality, values, interests). This is the layer ravanfix leans on. It's good for top-of-funnel engagement and retention, but it is explicitly **non-clinical** and must be walled off from routing so it never muddies the clinical rigor. Held to the same free/validated bar as the rest.

## What ravanfix actually offers — and the lesson to take (and not take)

ravanfix.com is a solo psychologist's content + booking site. Its questionnaire section is a set of engagement/lead-gen quizzes: **GHQ** (General Health Questionnaire), **Enneagram**, **NEO long-form** (Big Five), **Cattell 16PF**, **Holland** career interest, and **Sternberg "Love Story"** tests for men/women.

**Take this lesson:** breadth and *engaging* entry points work. People love personality, relationship, and career tests, they share them, and they pull traffic. A "take a quick test, get a meaningful result" hook is powerful.

**Do NOT take this:** several of those instruments are the wrong choice for a clinical product:
- **Enneagram** (and MBTI) are *not scientifically validated* — fine as pop-quiz engagement, unusable for routing.
- **GHQ, NEO-PI-R, and 16PF are copyrighted commercial instruments.** Using them in a commercial product means licensing fees and legal exposure. ravanfix's use of them doesn't mean you can.

So Reitti's move is: adopt the *engagement insight* (Type 3), but swap every copyrighted or pseudo-scientific instrument for a **free, validated equivalent**, and keep the clinical layers (Type 1 & 2) to a strict evidence + licensing standard.

## Type 1 — "Find your path" (screening / routing)

| Instrument | Measures | Items / time | Scoring & bands | Licensing | Routing signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PHQ-4** | Combined anxiety + depression, the universal quick screen | 4 · <1 min | 0–12; normal 0–2, mild 3–5, moderate 6–8, severe 9–12 | Free, no permission required | The front door. Overall band → suggested rung. ≥3 on the depression pair opens PHQ-9; ≥3 on the anxiety pair opens GAD-7 |
| **PHQ-9** | Depression severity | 9 · ~2 min | 0–27; cutoffs 5/10/15/20 | Public domain | Moderate → nettiterapia / groups; moderate-severe + long duration → individual / Kela. **Item 9 = self-harm → immediate crisis trigger** |
| **GAD-7** | Anxiety severity | 7 · ~2 min | 0–21; cutoffs 5/10/15 | Free, no permission required | Same band logic as PHQ-9 for the anxiety domain; high scores + panic descriptors → structured/CBT-style tags |
| **WHO-5** | General wellbeing (also a Type 2 tracker) | 5 · ~1 min | 0–100 (raw ×4); <50 low wellbeing, <28 = screen further | Free (WHO; acknowledge source) | Low wellbeing corroborates a moderate routing; gentle entry instrument for people wary of symptom questions |
| **AUDIT-C** | Alcohol use risk | 3 · <1 min | 0–12; risky ≥4 (men) / ≥3 (women) | WHO, free | Flags a substance dimension → adds substance-aware resources; not a rung by itself |
| **PC-PTSD-5** | Trauma screen (for the grief/loss/big-change domain) | 5 · <1 min | 0–5 yes/no; positive ≥3 | Public domain (US VA/NCPTSD) | Positive screen → trauma-informed therapy tag; routes toward appropriately trained providers |

**Design note:** a person never sees all six. PHQ-4 runs for everyone; the deeper screeners appear only when the quick screen (or the domain question) points to them. That is the whole "tiny first win → deeper only if flagged" funnel.

## Type 2 — "Track your progress" (measurement-based care)

| Instrument | Measures | Items / time | Scoring & bands | Licensing | Along-the-way use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WHO-5** | Wellbeing over time | 5 · ~1 min | 0–100 | Free | The default progress tracker; re-take every 2–4 weeks, show trend |
| **CBI** (Copenhagen Burnout Inventory) | Burnout (personal / work / client subscales) | 19 · ~4 min | 0–100 per subscale | Public domain (free alternative to the licensed MBI) | Track burnout recovery; share with therapist / occupational health |
| **CSI-4 / CSI-16** (Couples Satisfaction Index) | Relationship satisfaction | 4 or 16 · 1–3 min | Higher = more satisfied; CSI-16 cutoff ~51.5 | Free (Funk & Rogge). **Avoid** the licensed Dyadic Adjustment Scale | Track relationship strain across couples work |
| **ISI** (Insomnia Severity Index) | Insomnia severity | 7 · ~2 min | 0–28; 0–7 none, 8–14 sub-threshold, 15–21 moderate, 22–28 severe | **[verify commercial license]** (Morin) | Track sleep; often pairs with a CBT-I referral |
| **PSS-4 / PSS-10** (Perceived Stress Scale) | Perceived stress load | 4 or 10 · 1–2 min | Higher = more stress | Free for non-profit; **[verify commercial license]** | Track stress load between sessions |
| **UCLA-3** (Loneliness, short form) | Social connection / loneliness | 3 · <1 min | 3–9; higher = lonelier | Free | Track social connection; route toward groups/community |
| **ORS / SRS** (Outcome & Session Rating Scales) | Generic session-by-session outcome + therapeutic alliance | 4 + 4 · <1 min each | 0–40 each | **[verify commercial license]** (free for solo use; licensed for orgs) | The clinician's gold-standard measurement-based-care pair — worth evaluating once the clinician is aboard |

## Type 3 — "Understand yourself" (optional engagement layer)

Non-clinical, engaging, shareable. Walled off from routing. Every entry here replaces a copyrighted or pseudo-scientific ravanfix equivalent with a free, validated one.

| Instrument | Measures | Licensing | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Big Five via IPIP** (or BFI-2) | The five scientifically-supported personality dimensions | IPIP is public domain; BFI-2 free for non-commercial ([verify]) | The validated, free replacement for the copyrighted NEO-PI-R. Can lightly inform therapist-style matching |
| **RIASEC via O*NET Interest Profiler** | Career/vocational interests | Public domain (US DOL) | Free replacement for the copyrighted Holland instrument; good for the "life direction" audience |
| **ECR-R (short)** | Adult attachment style | Free (research) | Engaging *and* clinically useful for relationship work; feeds therapist context, not routing |
| **Values sort / PVQ** | Personal values | Free (Schwartz PVQ) | Excellent therapy-prep tool; helps a person articulate what matters before a first session |

**Deliberately excluded, and why (say this out loud to a clinician):** Enneagram and MBTI (not scientifically validated); NEO-PI-R, Cattell 16PF, GHQ, Maslach MBI, and the Beck inventories (copyrighted commercial instruments — licensing cost and legal risk). ravanfix uses several of these; Reitti does not.

## How each test feeds the routing engine

This is the statement to build the engine around, expanded into its inputs:

> **The routing engine maps (severity band + primary domain + duration + budget + language + trauma/substance flags) → a suggested ladder rung, plus the adjacent rungs shown as "if this feels like too much / not enough." Pure lookup against clinician-approved rules; printable for sign-off.**

The signal each layer contributes:
- **Severity band** (PHQ-4, then PHQ-9 / GAD-7): the primary intensity input. Higher band → higher rung.
- **Primary domain** (the "what brings you here" question + which deep-dive fired): tags the *kind* of care (burnout group vs. trauma-informed individual vs. sleep/CBT-I).
- **Duration**: recent strain biases toward lower rungs (self-help, groups, nettiterapia); long-standing biases toward individual / Kela.
- **Budget & language**: never filter care out — they only shift the *suggested* starting rung and the provider tags.
- **Safety flags** (PHQ-9 item 9, PC-PTSD-5): item 9 triggers crisis routing immediately; a positive trauma screen adds a trauma-informed tag.

Every rule is an explicit `if → then` line in config, reviewable by the clinician. No instrument ever outputs a diagnosis; it outputs a band and a tag that the rules turn into a suggestion.

## Presentation pattern (purpose now, science on demand)

Each test in the UI shows two layers, so a casual user isn't overwhelmed but a curious one can go deep:

1. **One-line purpose** (always visible): e.g. *"A quick read on how much pressure you're carrying and how well it eases between demands."*
2. **"About this test" (expandable):** what it measures, how it's scored, its validation background in plain terms, that it's screening not diagnosis, its item count and time, and its licensing/source. This is where the scientific credibility lives for the people who want it — and it's exactly what a clinician or a skeptical user will check.

Keep the purpose line human and the science section factual. Never show a clinical cutoff as a verdict; show a band plus a supportive reflection.
