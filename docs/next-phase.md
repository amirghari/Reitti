# Mielenreitti, next phase: the directory and the guides

Written for the Mielenreitti builder agent (repo github.com/amirghari/Reitti). Plan agreed with Amir on 6.11.2026. Read CLAUDE.md, docs/v2-plan.md and docs/v2-decisions.md first; this file adds to them and does not override them. Amir triggers every task; nothing here starts on its own.

## 1. The goal in one paragraph

Mielenreitti becomes the place in Finland where a person finds mental-health help: free and public help first (already built), and a directory of licensed professionals (psychotherapists, psychologists, psychiatrists) with city pages that rank on Google. People's own answers keep staying on their phone. Professionals get a page they can claim and fill in. Money comes later, from claimed profiles at a flat fee and from organisations that place the service, never from a per-session cut.

## 2. Rules that do not change

These come from docs/v2-decisions.md and Amir's words. A task that breaks one is wrong.

1. No accounts and no login for the people who use the questionnaire. Their answers never leave the phone. No server ever stores a person's answers or results.
2. No diagnosis, no single machine recommendation. Content and directory entries are never shown "because of your score". Content attaches to steps and topics, not to results.
3. The crisis path stays as it is: deterministic, always reachable, never AI.
4. No per-session take-rate, ever. No ads, ever.
5. The rules engine (packages/engine) stays pure and untouched by this work. packages/ai stays empty.
6. No scraping of clinic or practice websites (Terveystalo, Mehiläinen, Pihlajalinna, private practice sites). Directory data comes from official public registers and from the professionals themselves.
7. Everything clinical stays marked clinicianReviewed:false until a Finland-registered clinician signs. The directory and the guide pages do not need a clinician; the content library's instructional texts do.
8. Test every link before it ships. Never invent a professional, a licence, a price or a statistic.

## 3. What to build, in order

### Phase A: directory v1, static, from official sources (first)

**Who is listed.** Three types in one table, with a `type` field: `psychotherapist`, `psychologist`, `psychiatrist`. Also keep the existing public and NGO entries in the same directory with their existing `role` (care / gated-care / route).

**Sources, in this order:**
1. Kela's search for rehabilitative-psychotherapy providers (kuntoutuspsykoterapian palveluntuottajahaku). Public, official, exists for exactly this purpose. Kela is redesigning it in 2026; use whatever is public at the time of the task and record the date.
2. JulkiSoteri (julkisoteri, opened 27.10.2026, run by Lupa- ja valvontavirasto): private health-care providers and their service units. Contact details are added there later with consent; take only what is published.
3. Valvira's JulkiTerhikki for licence checks: one professional at a time, by hand or one request per entry with a pause. Never a bulk harvest. Record `licence_checked_on` per entry.

Before writing the first importer, read each source's terms of use and robots.txt and put a one-paragraph note per source in docs/directory-sources.md: what it allows, what we take, how often we refresh. If a source forbids automated reading, we do not read it automatically; Amir decides what to do by hand.

**Data model (one row per professional):**
`id, type, name, title (as licensed), languages[], city, region, methods[], kela_approved (bool), kela_checked_on, licence_checked_on, source, source_url, source_date, website, booking_url, phone, email, prices (free text, optional), accepting_new_clients (unknown / yes / no), claimed (bool), claimed_on, do_not_list (bool), notes_internal`.
No personal identity codes, no home addresses, no health information about the professional. Only what the public source shows plus what a professional later adds themselves.

**Storage for phase A:** a dataset in the repo (JSON or SQLite checked in, generated by the importers), built into static pages at deploy time, same as the current directory. No backend yet. This keeps the "no server touches people's data" promise simple and lets the pages ship this month.

**Pages:**
- One page per professional: `/ammattilaiset/<slug>` (fi), `/sv/...`, `/en/professionals/<slug>`. Shows the fields above, "Licence checked on <date>", "Source: Kela / JulkiSoteri", and two links: "Is this you? Claim this page" and "Ask for a correction or removal". No ratings, no reviews.
- City pages, generated: `/psykoterapeutit/<city>`, `/psykologit/<city>`, `/psykiatrit/<city>`, plus language filters (`?spoken=en`) reusing the existing /directory filter code. Each page has a short honest intro in fi/sv/en, the list, and one line pointing to the free steps for people who cannot pay.
- Sitemap entries for all of them. Titles and meta descriptions per page. JSON-LD (Person / MedicalBusiness, no ratings).

**Legal plumbing, built in from day one:**
- A visible notice on every professional page: where the data came from, the date, and how to object or be removed (GDPR Article 14 information duty). Removal requests are handled within 7 days by setting `do_not_list = true`; the page returns 410.
- A privacy-page section for the directory: purpose (helping people find licensed help), legal basis (legitimate interest), sources, retention (refreshed from the source; removed on request), rights.
- A refresh job that re-imports from the sources and marks entries no longer present as `stale`, hidden after 60 days.

**Definition of done for phase A:** importers with tests against saved sample pages (no live calls in tests); the dataset; the three page types in three languages; axe clean; sitemap; the sources note; the privacy section; a short docs/directory.md. Amir reviews the first 20 entries by hand before the pages go public.

### Phase B: claim your profile (needs a small backend)

A professional clicks "Claim this page", enters their email, gets a magic link, confirms they are the person (match on the email domain of their practice or a manual check by Amir for the first hundred), and can then edit: languages, methods, prices, availability, photo, booking link, a short text. Edits go live after Amir approves them in a simple review list.

This needs a backend: a small API and a Postgres database on Hetzner in Helsinki (same pattern as Tiski: one server, backups, no customer data of the questionnaire kind ever on it). The backend holds only professionals' public profile data and their login. It never receives anything from the questionnaire. Keep it in its own package (services/directory) so the rule "the engine has no I/O" stays enforceable.

Later, not now: a flat monthly fee for a claimed profile with extra fields. Free until there are at least 100 claimed profiles and real traffic.

### Phase C: guide pages (can run in parallel with A)

Four to six text pages, in English first, that answer the searches people actually type, each ending with one link to the questionnaire and one to the directory:
- How to get therapy in Finland in English
- Kela psychotherapy: who gets it and how to apply
- Psychologist without a referral in Finland: what it costs
- What to do while you wait for therapy
- Free mental-health help in Finland right now
- The first winter in Finland: light, sleep and mood (Amir's own experience, labelled as experience)

Rules: no numbers without a source link; no claims about outcomes; "offered in English" not "answers in English"; facts about Kela and public services checked against the official page on the day of writing, with the date in the page footer.

### Phase D: content library (after a clinician is named)

Topic pages for the most common problems in Finland: low mood, anxiety, burnout and work stress, sleep, loneliness, alcohol, the dark months. Text first, video later. Each page: what it is in plain words, what helps (linking to Mielenterveystalo's reviewed self-help programmes rather than rewriting them), when to seek more help, and the steps on the ladder. Amir's first-person texts are a separate, clearly labelled section ("what helped me"). Instructional content is published only after the clinician has read it.

### Optional, any time: history on the phone

The browser keeps the person's past results locally (IndexedDB), shown as "Your results: <dates>" on the same device, with "download as a file" and "delete all". Nothing is sent anywhere. This replaces the idea of user accounts and keeps the promise on the front page true.

## 4. Not in this phase

- User accounts or login for people using the questionnaire.
- Storing anyone's answers or results on a server.
- Scraping private clinic websites.
- Booking inside Mielenreitti, payments, commissions.
- Any content shown based on a score.
- Sweden and Norway. Same pattern later (Socialstyrelsen HOSP, Norway's HPR), Finland first.
